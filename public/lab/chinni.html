My dearest Chinni,

It has been five years since I got the easiest, luckiest yes of my life — and somehow you still make ordinary Tuesdays feel worth writing about. I've been meaning to put this in words for a while. Here it finally is.

"You make patience look like a love language."

I don't remember the first time "Chinni" slipped out — somewhere between a bad joke and a good one, your name softened into something only I get to say. Five years later, it still does the same thing it did the very first time: makes the room feel like ours, no matter which room we're in.

morning coffee, slightly burnt

Some of what I remember doesn't photograph well — the long drive nowhere we took on a Sunday, lost on purpose; the balcony in the rain with no umbrella, because you said the rain smelled like beginnings and I believed you; the 2 a.m. conversations about nothing, and everything, that always started too late and ended too soon.

the long drive nowhere

monsoon, no umbrella

five first anniversaries

"Home stopped being a place the day you became it."

I won't pretend every year of this was easy — there were stretches where we were just tired, or distracted, or bad at saying the right thing to each other at the right time. I don't want to skip past that part like it didn't happen. It did. And somehow we kept choosing this anyway.

This is the part I'm bad at saying out loud, so I'm writing it instead: thank you. For staying easy to love on the hard days. For the version of patience only you seem to have. For choosing this — choosing us — five years running.

"Every year with you has felt like the first, and also like I've known you forever."

Happy five years, Chinni.

Always yours, and completely.
